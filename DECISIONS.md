# DECISIONS.md

1. **Indexes.** On `employees`, I created a unique `(emp_code)` index for identity/409 enforcement, `(department, emp_code)` for filtered listing, and `(department, joined_on)` plus `(joined_on, department)` for headcount queries. On `attendance_logs`, I created a unique `(emp_code, date)` index for one-log-per-day enforcement and monthly/PATCH lookups, `(date: -1, emp_code: 1)` and `(status, date: -1, emp_code: 1)` for sorted pagination, `(emp_code, punch_in: -1)` for punch-out lookups, and `(date, late_minutes, emp_code)` for the leaderboard. I rejected an index on `history.at` because nothing queries by correction time.
2. **Punch-in race.** Both requests resolve the employee, truncate `punched_at` to whole seconds, compute the IST `date`, and call `insert_one` directly without check-then-insert. MongoDB's unique `(emp_code, date)` index guarantees one insert succeeds (`201`) while the other raises `DuplicateKeyError`, which my handler catches to return `409`.
3. **Ties.** The pipeline computes standard competition ranks via `$setWindowFields` with `$rank` sorted by `total_late_minutes: -1`, then filters `rank <= limit` and sorts by `total_late_minutes: -1, emp_code: 1`. Both tied employees share the cutoff rank, so both are returned.
4. **Headcount.** The aggregation starts on `employees` (`joined_on <= month_end`), performs a `$lookup` into `attendance_logs`, and unwinds with `preserveNullAndEmptyArrays: true`, counting distinct `emp_code`s via `$addToSet`.
5. **One thing you would change.** At 100x scale (~10M logs), I would maintain pre-aggregated daily department and monthly employee summary collections updated incrementally on write, avoiding full-month `$lookup` scans on read.
